Australian Healthcare Workforce Analysis

Examining Workforce Growth, Distribution, and Sector Trends (2015–2024)

1. Introduction & The Big Picture

Australia faces ongoing challenges in maintaining a balanced healthcare workforce to keep pace with population growth and regional demands. This visualization project explores how individual health professions have evolved, scaled, and shifted relative to one another over a decade.

2. Growth by Profession (Streamgraph)

The streamgraph below illustrates the macro-level expansion and structural shifts across various Australian healthcare professions from 2015 to 2024. Symmetrical centering allows for clear observation of relative volume changes over time.

This visualization analyzes multi-year quantitative records of annual practitioner counts categorized by healthcare professions to evaluate long-term sector growth and structural dominance shifts. Using a streamgraph idiom with stacked area marks and monotone interpolation, quantitative years are mapped to the horizontal axis, centered practitioner volumes are mapped vertically, and distinct nominal professions are distinguished through categorical color channels.

3. Workforce Regional Distribution (Proportional Symbol Map)

This map shows the concentration of total healthcare workers across Australian states and territories for 2024.

Designed to compare spatial densities and regional workforce concentrations across Australia, this proportional symbol map overlays spatial point locations with quantitative 2024 workforce totals. Geographic latitude and longitude position the symbols accurately across an Australian GeoJSON base map, while circle area size visually encodes practitioner magnitude.

4. Healthcare Practitioner Density per 100,000 Population (2021)

Normalizing raw practitioner headcounts against ABS 2021 Census state populations reveals the true density of healthcare coverage across Australian states and territories.

To evaluate healthcare supply relative to regional population size across Australian administrative boundaries, this choropleth map integrates state polygon geometries with derived quantitative practitioner rates per 100,000 residents. Visual encoding relies on a sequential blue color saturation scale mapped directly to normalized rate values to highlight regional coverage disparities.

5. Equal-Weight Regional Distribution (Multi-Hex Cartogram)

Geographic maps can distort data because larger states dominate physical space while smaller territories are difficult to compare. This multi-hex cartogram scales regional area proportionally to healthcare workforce volume, allowing an unbiased visual comparison.

This multi-hex cartogram compares practitioner counts across all administrative regions relative to workforce magnitude without perceptual size bias caused by geographic land area. Abstract multi-hex grid coordinates map regions to proportional cluster sizes combined with quantitative totals, utilizing proportional point marks and sequential blue color saturation.
